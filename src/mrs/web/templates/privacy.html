What we keep about you

{{ server_name }} is a private music player run by a person, not a company. This is everything it holds about the people who sign in, why, and how to make it stop.

The short version. A name you chose, the email on your Google account, and the settings and playlists you make. What you listen to is only remembered if you turn that on, and you can turn it off, take a copy, or delete the lot from Settings at any time.

What’s held

Your account
The name you gave when you signed up, the email address and profile picture on your Google account, a private identifier Google gives us for you, when you joined, and which version of this notice you agreed to. We use the email to recognise you when you come back and so the owner can tell who’s who. We never see your Google password.
Your settings and playlists
Equaliser, volume, your saved and playlist songs, and how you’ve set the player up. These are yours and live in your own folder.
What you play and skip (only if you opted in)
If you ticked “Learn what I like”, the server remembers which songs you play, skip and like so it can tune your radio and suggestions. If you didn’t, none of this is recorded against you, and there’s a switch in Settings to change your mind either way. Turning it off, or pressing “Forget what it has learned”, removes what it knows.
Usage counts
How many songs you’ve requested and how long you’ve listened, so the owner can see the place is being used. Counted against your account only if you opted in; otherwise it’s only added to the anonymous totals for the whole server.
A change log
A log the owner can read of changes made to the server itself, like settings. It isn’t a record of what you listen to. If a change was made from your account it’s marked with a code for your account, not your name, and kept for {{ audit_days }} days.
Technical logs
Operational logs on the server that keep the last few megabytes. They use a short code for your account, never your email or name.

Your rights, and how to use them

  • See it. Settings → Account → Download my data gives you everything above as a file.
  • Fix it. Change your name from the same place.
  • Stop the learning. Switch off “Learn what I like” whenever you want.
  • Delete it. Settings → Account → Delete my account removes your account, settings, playlists, taste and counts, and any change-log entries from your account. There’s no waiting period and no undoing it. If you can’t reach the player (say the owner has blocked you), the same download and delete buttons are on the page you land on.

Songs you added to shared playlists stay in those playlists, since other people are using them, but your name comes off them.
