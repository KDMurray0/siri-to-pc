Music Request

Ask for music. It works out the rest.

Type or say the name of a song, a band, an album or a mood. It finds it, plays it, and keeps the queue going on its own.

This takes about a minute. Everything in it is optional except the first step, and you can change any of it later in Settings.

Nothing here is sent anywhere. Your music, your history and your settings live in a folder on this PC. The only things that leave the machine are the searches and downloads themselves.
Step 1 of 4 · Required

Three programs do the actual work

This app is the part that decides what to play. Three well-known open-source tools do the playing and fetching, and they're installed with winget, which comes with Windows.

Only the missing ones are downloaded. Anything already on your PC is left exactly as it is.
Step 2 of 4 · Recommended

Signing in to YouTube

YouTube increasingly refuses to hand audio to anything that doesn't look like a signed-in browser. Without this, some songs download fine and others fail with "sign in to confirm you're not a bot".

YouTube session not checked

A copy of the session cookies from a browser you're already signed in with. It stays on this PC, it's never sent anywhere except back to YouTube, and you can delete it whenever you like.

If it isn't working, open Settings → Access after this and pick Sign in — it opens a normal Google login and keeps the result.

Happy to skip it? Plenty plays without it. Come back when something refuses to download.
Step 3 of 4 · Optional

Extras, all of them skippable

None of these are needed. Each one is off until you turn it on, and each says plainly what it wants.

Groq key free tier · better dictation

Reads messy phrasing — "put on that one by the bloke from Oasis" — and turns it into a search. There's a local parser that handles the ordinary cases perfectly well without it. A key is free from console.groq.com; it's stored on this PC and used only to interpret what you asked for.

Last.fm scrobbling

Records what you play to your Last.fm profile, and borrows your top artists once to give the radio a head start. Connect it and it asks Last.fm for permission in the normal way — this app never sees your password. Only what plays on this computer is scrobbled; guests on shared links never are.

Spotify links playlist import

Paste a Spotify or Apple Music playlist link into the search box and it matches the tracks up and plays them. Needs no account and no key — it only reads public playlists.

Turn any of these on later in Settings → Extras. Nothing here stops the app working.
Step 4 of 4 · Worth reading

Your key, and the links you hand out

There are two different credentials here and it's worth thirty seconds to know which is which.

  • The key is the master password for this server. It was generated on this PC when you first ran the app and it never leaves it. It's what the desktop player and your iPhone Shortcut use.
  • A link is something else: a signed pass that says who and for how long. Handing someone a link never hands them the key, and you can take any link back on its own without affecting anything else.
Links are how other people listen. Make one in Settings → Sharing. Choose their phone only and it plays on their device and nowhere else; choose any speaker and they can also drive this computer. Either way they get their own queue and never see your history.

Three wrong guesses from one address earns a 24-hour ban, links can be set to expire, and there's a red Revoke all button at the top of the Sharing tab for when you want everything back at once.

Ask it for something

Type a song, a band, an album or a vibe into the box at the top of the player. "Something like Radiohead", "the new Fontaines album", "quiet stuff for working" all work.

  • Settings → Sharing — links for other people, and who's listening right now.
  • Settings → Access — YouTube sign-in, if something won't play.
  • Settings → System — this guide again, any time.
